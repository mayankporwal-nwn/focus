# Research sources and editorial policy — FocusLock 2.0

Reviewed: 2026-10-30. These are content references, not clinical endorsements of this application.

## Evidence informing the app

- National Institutes of Health, **ADHD**: https://health.nih.gov/health-topics-a-z/adhd
  - ADHD has no established cure. Treatment often involves medication and/or psychotherapy and behavioral interventions. Helpful everyday supports include routines, exercise, rest, time management, visible reminders, and breaking tasks into steps.
- National Institute of Mental Health, **ADHD: What You Need to Know**: https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/attention-deficit-hyperactivity-disorder-what-you-need-to-know
  - Therapy and medication are evidence-based approaches; the app is *not* ADHD treatment.
- NICE guideline NG87, **ADHD diagnosis and management**: https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng87/chapter/recommendations
  - Adults with meaningful impairment need assessment and individualized treatment options, possibly including medication and/or psychological supports.
- NHS, **ADHD in adults**: https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/adhd-adults/
  - Exercise, regular sleep, balanced meals and accommodations may support management.
- NHS, **Insomnia**: https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/insomnia/
  - Most adults need approximately 7–9 hours of sleep. Sleep advice is not a treatment for every concentration problem.
- NHS, **Tiredness and fatigue**: https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/tiredness-and-fatigue/
  - Persistent cognitive difficulty or fatigue may have medical causes including sleep disorders, anemia and thyroid problems; a clinician can evaluate.
- World Health Organization, **Physical activity**: https://www.who.int/initiatives/behealthy/physical-activity
  - Adults should aim for at least 150 minutes/week of moderate activity; any amount is preferable to none. Exercise supports general health and cognitive well-being.
- Gollwitzer et al., **When and how of planning, meta-analysis of implementation intentions** (2024): https://doi.org/10.1080/10463283.2024.2334563
  - Supports the usefulness of rehearsed, specific if-then behavior plans. Does *not* imply ADHD can be cured.
- **Wellspent** randomized trial (2026), n=70 iPhone users, three weeks: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41950504/
  - Personalized social-media prompts reduced usage of the most problematic app by roughly 29 minutes/day in a short trial. It did *not* significantly reduce the primary measure of problematic social-media use; short trial and small population limit generalization. Our implementation is not this studied app.
- Android, **UsageStatsManager**: https://developer.android.com/reference/android/app/usage/UsageStatsManager
- Google Play, **AccessibilityService policy and prominent disclosure**: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10964491

## Editorial guardrails

- No claims to "cure ADHD", "detox the brain", treat disease, or eliminate brain fog.
- "Manifesting" is framed as **reflection + a measurable next action**. It does not alter external reality on its own and is not represented as medical science.
- Short breathing exercises, productivity sprints and journaling are optional self-management aids, not diagnostic or curative treatments.
- Seek medical attention for persistent or worsening brain fog, and urgent attention for sudden severe confusion or other acute neurological symptoms.
- Prompts emphasize autonomy and compassion, not guilt, shame, punitive streaks or certainty of outcome.
